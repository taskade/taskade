# Taskade vs Trello: An Honest Comparison (and Trello Alternative Guide)

If you're looking for a **Trello alternative**, there's a good chance you love how simple Trello is — and you've also hit its ceiling. Trello does one thing beautifully: boards and cards you can drag across columns. But the moment you need more views, want AI to actually do the work, or wish you could turn a board into a real app, you start reaching for add-ons and workarounds.

This page is written to be fair. Trello is a genuinely great product, and for a lot of people it's all they'll ever need. Below we lay out where each tool truly wins — Trello's elegant simplicity versus Taskade's AI-native depth — so you can choose based on how you actually work, not on a feature checklist.

If you want pure, dead-simple Kanban, Trello is hard to beat. If you want AI that does the work, more than one view, and a path to ship apps without code, Taskade fits better.

## Where Trello shines

We'll say it plainly: Trello is one of the best-designed productivity tools ever made. Its simplicity isn't a limitation to apologize for — it's the whole point, and it's done exceptionally well.

- **Dead-simple Kanban.** A board, some lists, and cards you drag from "To Do" to "Done." There is almost nothing to learn. You can be productive in your first five minutes, and so can everyone you invite.
- **The fastest onboarding in the category.** No hierarchy to design, no schema to configure, no setup wizard to suffer through. You open a board and start. For many teams, that immediacy is exactly why Trello stuck.
- **Power-Ups.** Trello's marketplace lets you bolt on calendars, integrations, custom fields, and more — only when you need them. You start minimal and add capability card by card, so the tool grows with you instead of overwhelming you on day one.
- **Butler automation.** Built-in, approachable automation with rules, card buttons, and scheduled commands. You can automate a surprising amount without leaving the board.
- **Visual clarity.** When your work genuinely *is* a pipeline — stages a card moves through — nothing communicates status faster than a Trello board at a glance.

If your work fits on a board and you want the lightest possible tool to manage it, Trello is a great answer. Don't switch away from something that works just to chase features you won't use.

## How they compare in practice

Feature tables only get you so far. Here's how the two tools tend to feel across the work people actually do.

**Starting a new project.**
In Trello, you make a board, add a few lists, and start dropping in cards. It's instant — there's genuinely nothing faster for a simple pipeline. In Taskade, you also start typing right away, but you can ask an agent to draft the whole structure for you, then flip between a Board, List, or Mind Map to see it the way that helps most. Trello gives you a board; Taskade gives you a board *and* six other ways to look at the same thing.

**Seeing your work differently.**
This is where the gap shows up first. A Trello board is a board — to see a calendar, a timeline, or a table, you add Power-Ups or move to a paid plan, and each view lives somewhat apart. In Taskade, every project is already all seven views at once. Need a deadline calendar this morning and a mind map this afternoon? One click, same data, no setup.

**Getting AI to help.**
Trello has limited native AI; meaningful assistance usually comes through Power-Ups or Atlassian's broader tooling. In Taskade, AI is the substrate. You can stand up a dedicated agent, train it on your own documents, choose its model, and give it tools so it can take actions — not just leave a comment. The mental model shifts from "cards I move myself" to "a teammate I assign work to."

**Automating a recurring process.**
Both tools automate, and Trello's Butler is genuinely good for board-level rules and buttons. Taskade adds an AI workflow generator: describe the process in a sentence and get a starting flow you can edit. If you like wiring card rules by hand, Butler is comfortable. If you'd rather describe the outcome, Taskade's generator shortens the path.

**Shipping something for people outside the team.**
This is where the products diverge most. Trello is for organizing work *inside* a team or for yourself. When you need a customer-facing app — a portal, a storefront, an intake tool with its own login and payments — Trello isn't built for that. Genesis is. You describe the app and it's hosted, with a database and auth, on a domain you can share.

## A fair word on simplicity vs. depth

This isn't a knock on either tool — it's a trade-off worth naming. Trello's strength is that it does one thing and does it cleanly. The flip side is that the moment your needs grow past a board, you start stacking Power-Ups, upgrading for views, and reaching for other tools to fill the gaps.

Taskade makes a different bet: keep the simple, board-style entry point, but build depth in from the start — seven views, agents, automations, and app-building, all native. That bet means there's more *available* than a single Trello board, even though you don't have to use it all on day one.

Neither bet is "right." If your work truly fits a board and you want the lightest tool that exists, Trello's focus is a feature, not a flaw. If you can already feel yourself outgrowing the board — wanting other views, real AI, or an actual app — Taskade gives you room to grow without switching tools again later.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Trello if:**

- Your work is a pipeline and a board captures it perfectly.
- You want the absolute simplest, fastest tool to set up and share.
- You're happy adding capability gradually through Power-Ups, and you don't need native AI.
- Onboarding speed for non-technical teammates matters more than depth.

**Choose Taskade if:**

- You want more than boards — List, Calendar, Table, Mind Map, Gantt, and Org Chart views over the same data.
- You want AI agents that actually do work — research, drafting, triage, execution.
- You want to ship apps without engineers, in weeks not months, with no code.
- You value a workspace where memory, agents, and automation live together natively, not as add-ons.

**Honest middle ground:** some people keep a Trello board for a specific, simple pipeline and use Taskade for everything that needs AI, multiple views, or app-building. There's no rule that says you must pick exactly one — match each tool to the job it's best at.

## FAQ

**Is Taskade a good Trello alternative?**
Yes — especially if you've outgrown a single board. Taskade includes a full Board view with drag-and-drop cards, so it covers what you love about Trello, and adds six more views (List, Calendar, Table, Mind Map, Gantt, Org Chart) plus AI agents, automations, and app-building. If you only ever want a board and nothing else, Trello's simplicity is hard to beat.

**Does Taskade have Kanban boards like Trello?**
Yes. Board view in Taskade gives you lists (columns) and cards you drag between them, just like Trello. The difference is that the board is one of seven views of the same project — switch to a calendar or table without rebuilding anything.

**Does Taskade do automations like Trello's Butler?**
Both automate. Trello's Butler offers approachable, board-level rules, buttons, and scheduled commands. Taskade offers visual automation flows plus an AI workflow generator that can build a flow from a plain-English description. Different style, overlapping outcomes.

**What can Taskade do that Trello can't?**
Three big things: **multiple native views** beyond boards, **AI agents** (single and multi-agent teams) that take real action, and **Taskade Genesis** — building a complete, hosted app (database, auth, payments, UI, custom domain) from a plain-English description. Trello organizes work; Genesis ships a standalone product. See [Genesis](https://www.taskade.com/blog/introducing-taskade-genesis).

**Is migrating from Trello hard?**
Most people start small: recreate one board in Taskade's Board view, try the other views and an agent on the same project, then expand. You don't have to move everything at once, and the board itself will feel familiar.

**Do I need to be technical to use Taskade?**
No. The whole point is plain English. If you can describe what you want — a board, an agent, an automation, or an app — you can build it.
